# 6.5.0

Fixes a stall seen at Super Awesome Games on 2026-11-10. Production Sprint 1 on Prism Break was planned but never started. Every `configure_delivery_plan` call failed with "Provide a name and distinct workflow tickets.", and two Producer commitments ("Preflight sprint …" and "Maintain delivery assignments and recover stopped tickets") repeated the same call every 7–10 minutes: about 1,000 capability calls in 40 minutes, with no one told.

Cause: the SDK named the increment plan `"Increment: "` plus the epic title, which came to 204 characters; the platform limit is 160. See SDK 5.16.0.

- Uses `CSweet.Agent.SDK` 5.16.0 (was 5.14.0). Increment plan names now fit the platform's limit, so the Prism Break plan saves on the next recheck. Already-created workflow tickets are reused.
- When the platform rejects the delivery plan itself (`DeliveryStaffingException.RejectedByPlatform`), the Producer messages its manager once per board and reason, with the platform's reason and the `Retry staffing: <what changed>` reply. The Video Game Director relays it to the CEO by the existing escalation convention. The commitment then rechecks every 60 minutes instead of every 15 or 20, so a fixed agent or platform still recovers on its own. Other staffing gaps keep their current waits.

No new permissions. Works with existing platforms. With a platform that predates retryable conflicts (csweet before 2026-11-10), a revision race on plan configuration can also be reported as a rejection: one extra message and a longer recheck, nothing worse.

Validation: not yet run. Written without a .NET SDK available; run `dotnet test` and the published output's `--self-test` before publishing. New tests: `DeliveryRejectionTests`.
